What are solfeggio frequencies?

Solfeggio frequencies is a name used for particular frequencies, including 528 Hz, in music and listening practices associated with meditation and relaxation. You may encounter them within a musical composition or as individual tones.

Hertz (Hz) means cycles per second. A 528 Hz tone vibrates 528 times each second. Lower numbers mean lower pitch; higher numbers mean higher pitch. Start by treating the number as a description of the sound you hear.

Compare a few tones at a similarly low, comfortable volume. Noticing how your listening experience compares with your expectations is part of discovering your preferences.

Solfeggio base tones available in Binawave

Binawave includes 174, 396, 528, 639 and 963 Hz base tones. A base tone sets the pitch delivered to the left ear. Pair it with a brainwave mode to create a binaural beat. These additional tones are available with Premium.

How is 528 Hz different from a binaural beat?

528 Hz describes a tone's pitch. A binaural beat frequency describes the difference between the tones delivered to your two ears. In Binawave, you choose these separately.

A Binawave setting exampleLeft 528 Hz − right 524 Hz = 4 Hz

A 528 Hz base tone paired with Theta mode creates a 4 Hz difference. Distinguish the pitch you hear from the rate of the perceived beat.

Keep the mode fixed while comparing base tones. Then keep the base tone fixed while changing modes. This makes each setting easier to explore.

A study of 528 Hz music with nine participants

In a 2018 study by Akimoto and colleagues, nine healthy adults listened to differently tuned piano music for five minutes on separate days. “528 Hz music” meant a composition tuned to a scale that included 528 Hz.

The researchers measured mood and salivary biomarkers before and after listening. In the 528 Hz condition, tension–anxiety scores fell, and a reduction in cortisol was among the reported changes. This small study explored responses to music through several measurements.

The experiment used piano music, rather than Binawave's individual tones or binaural beats. Knowing what was played helps put the findings in context while exploring the relationship between sound and mood.

Bring a solfeggio tone into your meditation routine

  1. Choose a pitch.Start with a tone that interests you and a low, comfortable volume through stereo headphones.
  2. Build your background.Gently layer rain, waves or another ambient sound you enjoy.
  3. Spend five minutes noticing.Let the sound stay in the background as you attend to breathing or sensations in your body.

Afterwards, notice one preference to explore next time: a different pitch or a quieter background layer. Let your sound routine develop around what you enjoy.
