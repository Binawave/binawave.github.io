Mindfulness means noticing the present moment.

Mindfulness involves attending to what is happening now without immediately judging it. Notice air moving through your nose, the chair supporting your body or a distant sound. Give everyday sensations a little more attention.

When a thought appears, notice it and return to your chosen sensation. The practice is the process of noticing and returning. Realizing that you were planning tomorrow is itself a moment of awareness.

How do mindfulness and meditation differ?

Meditation is a broad term for practices involving attention to breathing, sound and other experiences. Mindfulness describes a particular quality of attention: observing the present without rushing to judge it. Mindfulness meditation brings these together.

Sitting for five minutes and observing your breath is a meditation session. Noticing the warmth of tea or the sensations of walking can also bring mindfulness into daily life. A routine can include both dedicated sessions and small everyday moments.

A five-minute mindfulness meditation for beginners

  1. Sit comfortably.Find a supported posture. Close your eyes or keep them gently open.
  2. Set aside five minutes.Use a timer and choose one sensation to observe.
  3. Notice natural breathing.Let the breath stay natural. Attend to your nose, chest or abdomen—wherever it is easiest to feel.
  4. Notice and return.When you become aware of a thought, return to the breath. There is no need to count returns or score your performance.
  5. Finish gently.Notice surrounding sounds and your body before moving on.

Afterwards, recall one thing you noticed, such as a sound or the feeling of sitting. Choosing a familiar time for your next short session can help build a routine.

Using binaural beats and ambient sound for meditation

If you enjoy sound, start with a quiet background that leaves room for attention to breathing. Rain or waves can form a backdrop; a simple tone can also become an object of attention. You might choose to notice the sound itself during a session.

Binaural beats use slightly different pitches in your two ears to create a perceived beat at their difference. Use stereo headphones at a comfortable low volume. Binawave pairs base tones with brainwave modes and lets you layer ambient sounds.

Sound is one way to personalize your practice environment. Noticing when attention wanders and bringing it back remains your own practice.
