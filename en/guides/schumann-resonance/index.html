Schumann resonance

Schumann resonance begins between Earth and the ionosphere.

Above Earth lies the ionosphere, a region containing electrically charged particles. Picture the surface and the ionosphere enclosing a vast spherical space. Some electromagnetic waves created by lightning travel around Earth within it.

Waves that fit this space reinforce one another at particular frequencies: Schumann resonances. A musical instrument offers a useful analogy—a space has patterns that resonate especially well. Here, however, the waves are electromagnetic rather than vibrations in air.

NASA describes this extremely low-frequency phenomenon as a tool for studying Earth's atmospheric and electrical environment.

What does 7.83 Hz mean?

7.83 Hz is a representative value for the fundamental Schumann resonance. Hertz means cycles per second, so it corresponds to about 7.83 cycles each second. Additional resonances occur at higher frequencies.

The natural phenomenon is observed in Earth's electromagnetic environment. In a sound app, 7.83 Hz can instead be a setting used to generate audio. Understanding what is oscillating lets you appreciate both the physical phenomenon and the listening experience.

Binawave's Schumann mode creates a 7.83 Hz tone difference.

Schumann mode is a Premium binaural beat setting inspired by Earth's resonance. It places a 7.83 Hz difference between the tones delivered to your ears.

An example with a 528 Hz base toneLeft 528 Hz − right 520.17 Hz = 7.83 Hz

The audible tones are 528 Hz and 520.17 Hz. Their difference sets the rate of the perceived binaural beat.

Your phone delivers sound; it is not a device for reproducing Earth's electromagnetic environment. Enjoy the mode as an audio interpretation of a natural rhythm, perhaps layered with rain or waves.

Use Schumann mode as a background for meditation.

  1. Choose Schumann mode with Premium.Pair it with a base tone you enjoy.
  2. Keep the ears separate.Use stereo headphones at a low, comfortable volume.
  3. Add a natural sound.Gently layer waves, wind or another ambient sound.
  4. Attend to breathing.Try five minutes noticing the sensations of breathing in and out, with the sound in the background.

Changing the base tone or background layer changes the listening experience while the 7.83 Hz difference remains fixed. Let your own comfort guide the choice, and turn curiosity about nature into a moment for yourself.

Are Schumann resonance and solfeggio frequencies the same?

Schumann resonance is an electromagnetic phenomenon in nature. Solfeggio frequencies is a name used for particular audio pitches. Binawave lets you combine a base tone such as 528 Hz with the 7.83 Hz Schumann mode: pitch and tone difference are separate choices.
